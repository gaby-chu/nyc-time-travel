Editor's Note

Before your block was yours, it was someone else's first home in America.

For anyone who has ever wondered who lived on their block before them.

Name a New York City neighborhood and step back through its records: what its streets looked like around 1900–1920, 1940, 1960, 1980 and today; which immigrant communities arrived, peaked and moved on; and which of their buildings and institutions are still standing.

Type a question in the box, in your own words. You can ask any of these:

  • Then & now: what did it look like in each era?
  • Who settled, and when: which groups arrived, peaked and moved on?
  • What remains: which buildings and institutions survive?

Or say "tell me the story of <neighborhood>" and get all three.

Behind the scenes: an editor agent plans the research and sends several reporter agents out in parallel to historical newspapers, the New York Times, NYPL's digital collections, landmark designation reports and the census. A writer turns what they find into the edition below, with every fact linked to its source. It goes to press in about 4 minutes.

The Newsroom Live while the paper is being written

Who wrote this edition, and how. The Assignment Desk (Clarifier) works out the neighborhood, the Editor (Lead) plans the research and sends out reporters, the Staff Reporters (Researchers) work in parallel, and the Rewrite Desk (Writer) writes it up. Each card is one agent's notebook: its full context, one page per message, from its standing orders and assignment to its notes, tool calls and what each source sent back. Reporters working at the same time open their notebooks at the same time, each starting blank: a fresh context with only its own assignment. When the edition is filed, each card also lists every tool call that agent made, with its arguments and result.
