### Overview

Downtown Flushing evolved from a historic town with suburban estates into a dense commercial and transit hub, catalyzed by the arrival of the Queensboro Bridge and the IRT Flushing Line subway in the early twentieth century ([LPC, 1986](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1798.pdf)). While it thrived as a major department store retail center in the mid-twentieth century ([The New York Times, 1965](https://www.nytimes.com/1965/12/21/archives/retailers-study-sites-in-flushing-big-store-chains-reported-seeking.html)), a rapidly growing Asian presence by the late 1980s drove commercial development, transforming the neighborhood into a major Asian American shopping destination with extensive Chinese business signage ([The New York Times, 1990](https://www.nytimes.com/1990/12/23/realestate/perspective-downtown-flushing-growing-asian-presence-inspires-a-hotel.html), [The New York Times, 2025](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/07/23/nyregion/tangram-mall-flushing-queens-nyc.html)). ### Then & now

#### 1900-1920
During the 1900–1920 era, downtown Flushing evolved from a historic town with suburban estates into a developing urban center within the newly consolidated Borough of Queens ([LPC, 1965](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/0139.pdf)). [...]

#### 1940
In 1940, Downtown Flushing was rapidly transitioning from a semi-rural horticultural and truck-farming village into a commercial and residential hub ([LPC, 1986](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1798.pdf)). [...]

#### 1960
By late 1965, Downtown Flushing was operating as a thriving retail hub, with major department store chains such as Macy's, Abraham & Straus, and Alexander's seeking or inspecting new outlets and sites in the neighborhood ([The New York Times, 1965](https://www.nytimes.com/1965/12/21/archives/retailers-study-sites-in-flushing-big-store-chains-reported-seeking.html)). #### 1980
The Main Street and Roosevelt Avenue commercial district was undergoing physical transitions, as seen in an [about-1985 tax photo of 37-20 Main Street](https://nycrecords.access.preservica.com/uncategorized/IO_1a392574-32fc-4317-ad3e-545ce79ae5bb/) showing the structure and streetscape there ([NYC Department of Records](https://nycrecords.access.preservica.com/uncategorized/IO_1a392574-32fc-4317-ad3e-545ce79ae5bb/)). By the late 1980s, a growing Asian presence was driving commercial development, which inspired new construction such as a hotel ([The New York Times, 1990](https://www.nytimes.com/1990/12/23/realestate/perspective-downtown-flushing-growing-asian-presence-inspires-a-hotel.html)). This rapid development brought both community shifts and local discord to the neighborhood ([The New York Times, 1990](https://www.nytimes.com/1990/04/12/nyregion/development-brings-discord-to-flushing.html)). Additionally, traditionally insular regional groups began reaching out during this transitional era ([The New York Times, 1990](https://www.nytimes.com/1990/10/28/weekinreview/the-region-a-traditionally-insular-community-begins-to-reach-out.html)).
