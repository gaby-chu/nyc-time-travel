### Overview

Jackson Heights transformed from Newtown's undeveloped Trains Meadow farmland into a planned cooperative community designed for middle- and upper-class families in the early 20th century ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)). Over the subsequent decades, the neighborhood evolved into one of the city's most diverse global residential hubs, with over 57% of its population today born outside of the United States ([Census Bureau ACS 2020-2024](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B05006?g=1400000US36081027301,36081027302,36081027500,36081027701,36081027702,36081027900,36081028100,36081028300,36081028500,36081028700,36081028900,36081029100,36081030903,36081030904,36081030905,36081030906,36081032700,36081033700,36081033900,36081035100)). This shift was propelled by successive waves of international immigration, primarily from South America—notably Colombia and Ecuador—and South Asia—including India, Bangladesh, and Nepal—creating distinct commercial corridors and cultural enclaves that share the neighborhood's original historic garden apartment fabric ([Census Bureau ACS 2020-2024](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B05006?g=1400000US36081027301,36081027302,36081027500,36081027701,36081027702,36081027900,36081028100,36081028300,36081028500,36081028700,36081028900,36081029100,36081030903,36081030904,36081030905,36081030906,36081032700,36081033700,36081033900,36081035100); [The New York Times, 2020](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/08/27/arts/design/jackson-heights-queens-virtual-walk-tour.html)).

### Then & now

#### 1900-1920
In the early 20th century, Jackson Heights was developed on an extensive tract of farmland formerly known as the Trains Meadow section of Newtown in northwest Queens ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)). [...] The developers introduced innovative "garden apartment" and "garden home" building types that treated entire rectangular blocks as single planning units with shared interior gardens ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)). [...]

#### 1940
By 1940, Jackson Heights had established a cohesive, low-rise landscape of garden apartments, multi-family apartment buildings, and semi-detached homes ([NYC OpenData, 1940](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/gpmc-yuvp.json)). [...]

#### 1960
During the post-war era, six-story brick apartment complexes designed by prominent architects like Philip Birnbaum, such as Roosevelt Terrace, were constructed primarily along 35th Avenue ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)). [...]

#### 1980
By the late 1980s, Jackson Heights was characterized by tree-lined avenues lined with brick, slate, and stucco apartment buildings as well as one-, two-, and three-family homes ([The New York Times, 1987](https://www.nytimes.com/1987/12/27/realestate/if-youre-thinking-of-living-in-jackson-heights.html)). [...]

#### Today
Today, Jackson Heights is a high-density global neighborhood defined by bustling public spaces, active street life, and commercial strips on 74th Street, 37th Avenue, and Roosevelt Avenue, including Diversity Plaza ([The New York Times, 2020](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/08/27/arts/design/jackson-heights-queens-virtual-walk-tour.html)). [...] The historic fabric of classic six-story brick garden apartments with private rear courtyards exists directly alongside these active immigrant-run commercial hubs ([The New York Times, 2020](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/08/27/arts/design/jackson-heights-queens-virtual-walk-tour.html)).

### What remains

Jackson Heights retains much of its highly unified architectural layout ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)). [...]

Surviving physical developments include:
* **Innovative Garden Apartment Complexes:** Original complexes, such as **Linden Court** (1919–21, Andrew J. Thomas), **Hawthorne Court** (1921, George H. Wells), **The Chateau** (1922, Thomas), **The Towers** (1923–25, Thomas), and **Greystone Apartments** (1916–18, George H. Wells), remain intact ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)). [...]
* **Cooperative Living Structures:** These early garden apartments continue to operate under their historic cooperative ownership structures, and residents manage the shared private inner greenways ([The New York Times, 2016](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/05/22/realestate/co-op-wars-do-you-dare-walk-on-the-grass.html)).
* **Architectural Diversity:** The neighborhood retains diverse historic building facades, including neo-Georgian styles like the Colonial Apartments (1915–16), French, English, and Spanish vernaculars ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf); [The New York Times, 2015](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/05/24/realestate/jackson-heights-queens-diverse-and-evolving.html)).
- [...]

The vast undeveloped farmland formerly known as Newtown's Trains Meadow section is gone, completely built over during the 20th century under the direction of the Queensboro Corporation ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf)).

### What the records don't show

Available historical records present thin data regarding Jackson Heights' mid-to-late 20th-century transitions, particularly during the decades when it changed from a middle-class residential stronghold in 1960 to a diverse global neighborhood in 1980 ([LPC, 1993](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1831.pdf); [The New York Times, 1987](https://www.nytimes.com/1987/12/27/realestate/if-youre-thinking-of-living-in-jackson-heights.html)).
